# M5.6: Apple On-Device Engines (SpeechAnalyzer and Foundation Models), Detailed Plan

**Goal:** on macOS 26 and later, let the whole pipeline run on Apple's own on-device models, with nothing for us to download:
**SpeechAnalyzer** as a speech engine next to Whisper, and Apple's **on-device language model** (the Foundation Models
framework, Apple Intelligence) as a cleanup engine next to Claude, the API and S1-mini. Audio and text stay on the Mac.
Whisper, Claude and S1-mini keep working exactly as today, and macOS 13–25 sees no change.

**Status legend:** ☐ to do · ◐ in progress · ☑ done

**Status: ◐ built and measured (2026-10-18), on the branch `m5.6-apple-on-device`; a real dictation in the app is the last check.** This takes the Apple speech engine out of the
M5.5 plan's v0.6.0 ([§3F](M5.5-command-mode-context-snippets.md#f-apple-speech-engine-v060), tasks C14–C18) and adds Apple's
language model. On-screen context and snippets move to the release after it.

## 2. What the research found

Sources are in [§10](#10-sources). ✅ = Apple's documentation or tested on the macOS 27 Mac; 🟡 = third-party reports.

**SpeechAnalyzer (Speech framework, macOS 26+)**
- ✅ Two transcribers:
  - **`SpeechTranscriber`** is the new long-form model. Its output is punctuated. Its presets are `.transcription` and
    `.progressiveTranscription`; the WWDC25 names `.offlineTranscription` and `.progressiveLiveTranscription` never shipped.
  - **`DictationTranscriber`** is the older system-dictation model. Its presets are `.longDictation`,
    `.progressiveLongDictation` and others, and it has a `.punctuation` option.
- ✅ **Vocabulary:** only `DictationTranscriber` is documented to use `AnalysisContext.contextualStrings` (at most 100 phrases
  of one or two words). 🟡 Developers report `SpeechTranscriber` accepts them and ignores them. Spike A1 compares both.
- ✅ **Audio:** the analyzer doesn't convert audio. Buffers must be in `bestAvailableAudioFormat(compatibleWith:)` (nil until
  the language model is installed), or the result is silently empty. A file (`analyzeSequence(from: AVAudioFile)`) is
  converted for us. macOS 27 adds `AnalyzerInputConverter`.
- ✅ **Language models** are downloaded through `AssetInventory.assetInstallationRequest(supporting:)`, with a `Progress`.
  macOS stores them, shares them between apps, updates them, and may remove one that hasn't been used for a while. They run
  outside our process, so they don't count towards our memory.
- ✅ **No Speech Recognition permission.** Apple's page says the authorization "only applies to `SFSpeechRecognizer`".
  🟡 Unbundled command-line tools use SpeechAnalyzer without it, e.g. [yap](https://github.com/finnvoor/yap). Only the
  microphone permission applies, and only to the process that records: our app, as today.
- 🟡 **Accuracy and speed:**
  - Word error rate is about Whisper large-v3-turbo's (LibriSpeech: 2.1% clean, 4.6% other).
  - About 2× faster than MacWhisper turbo on a 34-minute file.
  - Fed live while recording, the final text is ready 20–80 ms after stop.
  - Proper nouns are weak, as with Whisper.
- 🟡 **Languages:** about 22 languages (42 locales), against Whisper's ~100.

**Foundation Models (macOS 26+)**
- ✅ **API:** `SystemLanguageModel(useCase: .general, guardrails: .permissiveContentTransformations)`, `.availability`
  (unavailable: `deviceNotEligible`, `appleIntelligenceNotEnabled`, `modelNotReady`), `LanguageModelSession(model:instructions:)`,
  `prewarm()`, `respond(to:options:)` with `GenerationOptions(sampling: .greedy)`.
- ✅ **Context:** 4,096 tokens on macOS 26. Measured on this Mac with macOS 27: about 8,192 (7,501 tokens worked, 9,001 failed).
  🟡 That's said to need an M3 or later with 12 GB+. Our system prompt is about 1,700 tokens, so either fits.
- ✅ **Measured on this Mac** with our `prompts/system.md`, through Apple's `fm` CLI:
  - 1.2–2.0 s per cleanup, including process start.
  - Self-corrections and technical terms were right.
  - **"Write me an email to John…" came back as an email**, not as the cleaned-up sentence. Prompt tuning, the eval and the
    meaning guard are needed before it's trusted.
- ✅ **Permissive guardrails** never throw `guardrailViolation` for string output, but the model "may still refuse",
  returning the refusal *as text*, so the helper must catch it.
- ✅ **macOS 27** deprecates `LanguageModelSession.GenerationError` in favour of `LanguageModelError`. The new model is
  "rebuilt from the ground up" with better instruction following. Every OS update changes the model, so prompts are versioned.
- ✅ **Distribution:**
  - No entitlement is needed for the on-device model.
  - 🟡 apfel, an unbundled Homebrew CLI built with CLT only, runs it fine.
  - 🟡 `@Generable`/`@Guide` need the `FoundationModelsMacros` plugin, which only Xcode ships. Plain `String` responses need
    no macros, which is all cleanup needs.
- ⚠️ **Apple's `/usr/bin/fm` CLI isn't used.** Its license (`fm license --show`) says not to "programmatically access or use
  Apple models through Apple software or services except as expressly permitted", and each user would have to accept it
  first. The framework, called from our own helper, is the sanctioned route. Apple's
  [acceptable use requirements](https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/acceptable-use-requirements-for-the-foundation-models-framework/)
  apply. Using Apple's own permissive guardrail setting isn't "circumventing guardrails".

## 3. Design

### The helper: `ovt-apple`

One small Swift command-line program does everything that needs the macOS 26 SDK. It's bundled in `Contents/Helpers` like
`whisper-server`, and both the app and `dictate.sh` run it.

- **Its own SwiftPM package** in `app/AppleHelper/`, with `swift-tools-version:6.2`, `platforms: [.macOS("26.0")]`
  and Swift 5 language mode. The main app's package is untouched: still `swift-tools-version:5.9`, macOS 13, no new
  frameworks, no `#if compiler` blocks. A toolchain older than Swift 6.2 (the `macos-14` CI job, older Macs) builds the app
  without the helper, and the Apple options don't appear.
- `build-app.sh` builds it when `swift --version` is ≥ 6.2, copies it to `Contents/Helpers` and signs it with the rest.
  `BundledHelpers` copies it out of the bundle like the others (quarantine).
- Running the helper on macOS 13–25 isn't possible (it needs macOS 26), so the app and the script only launch it after
  checking `ovt-apple status` on macOS 26+ (`sw_vers`, cached).

| Command | Does | Output |
|---|---|---|
| `ovt-apple status [--locale en]` | Speech: `SpeechTranscriber.isAvailable`, the locale's asset status. Language model: `availability` and its reason, `contextSize` | One JSON line: `{"speech":{"available":true,"locale":"en_US","assets":"installed"},"llm":{"available":false,"reason":"appleIntelligenceNotEnabled"}}` |
| `ovt-apple speech-install [--locale en]` | `AssetInventory` download | `progress 0.42` lines, then `installed`; exit 1 with a reason |
| `ovt-apple transcribe [--locale en] [--transcriber speech\|dictation] [--vocab-file F] <wav>` | Analyzes a finished WAV | The text on stdout |
| `ovt-apple transcribe … --pcm` | Live: prepares the analyzer, prints `ready` on stderr, reads 16 kHz mono 16-bit PCM from stdin while you speak, converts it with `AVAudioConverter`, finalizes at end of input | The text on stdout |
| `ovt-apple cleanup --instructions-file F` | `prewarm()` with the instructions as soon as it starts, then reads the user message on stdin and responds (greedy, permissive guardrails) | The text on stdout. Failures: exit 2 and one line on stderr: `unavailable <reason>`, `refusal`, `guardrail`, `context`, `rate-limited`, `error <detail>` |

The `cleanup` command prewarms before reading stdin, so `dictate.sh refine` can start it while you speak, the way it pre-starts
Claude. If spike A2 shows no gain, it's started after the transcript instead. A refusal returned as text (permissive
guardrails) is caught: an answer that starts like a refusal ("I'm sorry", "I can't assist") while the transcript doesn't counts
as `refusal`.

### Speech: the Apple engine

```
recording starts ─> speech engine = Apple (and available)? skip `whisper-server start`
                 ─> dictate.sh transcribe-stream  ─exec─>  ovt-apple transcribe --pcm  (prepares the analyzer)
                 ─> each Recorder buffer: to the WAV as today, and its 16 kHz Int16 bytes to the helper's stdin
stop             ─> close stdin ─> the helper finalizes ─> transcript (about 0.1 s)
                 ─> empty with exit 0: "No speech"; failed: Whisper on the WAV, if a Whisper model is installed
                 ─> `dictate.sh refine` as today
```

- **One more `dictate.sh` command:** `transcribe-stream` runs the helper with the app's stdin and logs
  `TRANSCRIBE engine=apple audio=…s apple=…ms` like Whisper's line. It applies the same hallucination filter and the
  `MIN_SECONDS` rule (from the byte count).
- **`transcribe <wav>` picks the engine** from `SPEECH_ENGINE` (`whisper` | `apple`; `VTT_SPEECH_ENGINE` from the app).
  With `apple` it runs `ovt-apple transcribe <wav>` and falls back to Whisper when that fails. So `dictate start`,
  `dictate file`, `selftest` and the evals can use Apple without the app.
- **The app** (Swift 5.9, no new SDK):
  - `Recorder` gets an `onPCM` callback with the converted bytes it already writes to the WAV.
  - `Dictation` keeps a `ScriptRun` for the stream, like the pre-started `refine`, and writes to its stdin on a serial queue.
  - The WAV is always written, so the Whisper fallback and the CLI path work.
  - Cancel closes the stream with nothing; the helper exits.
- **Language:** `LANGUAGE` (`en`, `de`, …) maps to a locale through `SpeechTranscriber.supportedLocale(equivalentTo:)`;
  `auto` uses the system language. A language Apple doesn't have shows "Not available for <language>" in Settings and
  uses Whisper.
- **Vocabulary:** passed as contextual strings when the transcriber uses them (spike A1). Names otherwise rely on the
  dictionary replacements and the cleanup's `<vocabulary>`, as with S1-mini.
- **Settings → Speech:** an engine picker, "Whisper, on this Mac" or "Apple, on this Mac", shown only when `ovt-apple status`
  says speech is available. Below it, the Apple language model row: installed, or Download with progress.
- **Setup:** on macOS 26+, if spike A1 makes Apple the default, the speech step offers Apple first and skips the Whisper
  download.

### Cleanup: the Apple engine

- `CLEANUP=apple` (`VTT_CLEANUP=apple`). `refine_apple()` sends the same system prompt (core rules plus the mode prompt) and
  the same user message (context, vocabulary, transcript) as Claude, through `ovt-apple cleanup`. `APPLE_TIMEOUT` is 10 s.
- **Prompt:** the same `prompts/system.md`. If the eval shows the small model answering requests, an Apple-only addition goes
  in `prompts/apple.md`, appended for this engine only, so Claude's prompt and results don't change.
- **Fallback:** the setting stays "Use an offline fallback" (`S1_FALLBACK` / `VTT_S1_FALLBACK`, kept for compatibility). When
  the online engine is offline or fails, the fallback is Apple when it's available, otherwise S1-mini (`FALLBACK_ENGINE=auto`,
  or `apple` / `s1` to force one). If Apple fails, S1-mini is tried when installed, then the raw text.
- **The contract:**
  - `REFINE_STATUS` gains `apple` and `apple-fallback`, and `ENGINE` gains `apple`.
  - Exit 4 now means "an on-device engine replaced the online engine". The app reads which one from the result file's
    `engine` field ("cleaned offline by Apple Intelligence" or "by S1-mini").
  - The meaning guard runs on `apple` and `apple-fallback`, as on S1-mini.
  - Engine errors use the existing `ENGINE_ERR_FILE`; `unavailable` maps to `config`.
- **Settings → Cleanup:** "Apple Intelligence, on this Mac" in the engine picker when available, with the reason when it isn't
  ("Turn on Apple Intelligence in System Settings", "This Mac isn't eligible", "The model is still downloading").
- **Setup:** with no Claude and Apple Intelligence on, Apple is selected instead of S1-mini (no 484 MB download).
- **Not for Command Mode:** Command Mode stays on Claude or the API. Apple's model may be good enough later; that needs the
  command eval.

### The CLI, the evals and the tests

- `config.example.sh`: `SPEECH_ENGINE`, `CLEANUP=apple`, `FALLBACK_ENGINE`, `APPLE_HELPER` (default: the bundled copy, then
  `ovt-apple` on `PATH`), `APPLE_TIMEOUT`. `install.sh` doesn't build the helper: the CLI uses it when the app is installed.
- `evals/run.py --cleanup apple` and `--e2e --engine apple|whisper`.
- `scripts/test-dictate.sh` gets a fake `ovt-apple` (`scripts/testdata/fake-ovt-apple`) covering:
  - transcribe, stream and fallback to Whisper;
  - cleanup, unavailable, refusal and timeout;
  - the fallback choice between Apple and S1-mini;
  - exit 4 with `engine=apple`.
  
  On Linux the Apple paths are never taken (the `OS` check).
- `selftest` runs the Apple engines too when they're available.

### Logs

`APP TIMING` gains `engine=whisper|apple`; `TRANSCRIBE` and `REFINE` lines name the engine. No dictated text without `LOG_TEXT=on`.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

- **Speech:**
  - On macOS 26+, with Apple selected, a dictation is transcribed with no Whisper model installed and no `whisper-server` running.
  - Stop to text is no slower than with Whisper.
  - If Apple fails, the dictation uses Whisper (when installed) or says so. A dictation is never lost.
- **Cleanup:**
  - With Apple Intelligence selected, a dictation is cleaned up on the Mac with no network.
  - With Claude selected and the network off, the fallback is Apple, and the overlay says "cleaned offline".
  - "Write me an email to John…" comes back as that sentence.
  - The meaning guard catches a dropped number or "not".
- **Compatibility:**
  - On macOS 13–15, or with a toolchain older than Swift 6.2, nothing changes and no Apple option appears.
  - Linux is unaffected.
  - `scripts/test-dictate.sh`, shellcheck, the build, the snapshots and the self-test pass.
  - The cleanup eval with Claude stays 25/25.
- The e2e eval results for both speech engines and the cleanup eval for Apple are recorded in §8.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Apple's language model answers dictated requests instead of cleaning them up | The eval's safety cases, `prompts/apple.md`, the meaning guard; it's an option and a fallback, never the default over Claude |
| Apple speech is worse on names and technical terms | The default switches only if the eval says so; dictionary replacements and the cleanup's vocabulary still fix names |
| CLT 27's SwiftUI macros break the main app's build | Spike A0 first; the `typealias` workaround; CI keeps an older-toolchain job |
| The model changes with every OS update | Prompt versions in `prompts/apple.md`; the eval re-run after a macOS update |
| Smaller context (4,096) on M1/M2 and on macOS 26 | Prompt ~1,700 tokens plus a dictation fits; over the limit is a `context` error and the fallback |
| macOS removes an unused speech language model | `status` before each dictation (cached for a minute); a missing model means Whisper, and Settings offers the download |
| A helper launched by a menu-bar app is rate-limited as background | Spike A2; a `rate-limited` error falls back to S1-mini or raw text |

## 8. Results

Measured on 2026-10-18 on the development Mac: macOS 27.0.1, M3 Pro, 18 GB, Command Line Tools 27.0 (Swift 6.4,
macOS 27 SDK).

**A0: toolchain.**
- A Command Line Tools install over the old one left stale 2023 `PackageDescription` interfaces behind, and every manifest
  failed to link. A clean reinstall (`sudo rm -rf /Library/Developer/CommandLineTools` first) fixed it.
- The package manifest needs `swiftLanguageModes`.
- As reported, Swift 6.4 makes `@State` a macro whose plugin ships only with Xcode. The app now uses `@StoredState` (a
  typealias for `SwiftUI.State`), and builds, snapshots, the window test and the logic self-test (17/17) pass.

**A1: speech.**
- Apple's English model was already on the Mac (macOS uses it for dictation), so "downloading" took 0.2 s.
- No permission prompt appeared, from the terminal or from the app.
- The locale needed our own matching: for this Mac's en-BD, `supportedLocale(equivalentTo:)` returned en-CA for one
  transcriber and en-NZ for the other. It now picks the system locale if supported, else the language's main region
  (en-US).
- End-to-end eval, the 8 cases with speech: synthesized with `say`, then transcribed, then cleaned up with Claude Haiku,
  `--jobs 1`.

  | Speech engine | Passed | Stop → transcript (median) | Stop → cleaned (median) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Whisper large-v3-turbo (whisper-server) | 7/8 | 1,230 ms | 2,528 ms |
  | Apple `SpeechTranscriber`, finished WAV | 7/8 | 504 ms | 1,794 ms |
  | Apple `DictationTranscriber` + vocabulary, finished WAV | 7/8 | 1,049 ms | 2,465 ms |

  All three fail the same case (spoken email addresses).
- Apple's raw text is clearly worse on technical terms: "posters" (Postgres), "main dog pie" (main.py), "www.git.com",
  "pingsupportedcompany.org", "Cubernates". Whisper's style prompt gets these right. Claude repairs most of them; an
  offline cleanup would not.
- **Live:** the speech fed at real-time pace through `transcribe-stream` (12.7 s) had its final text **79 ms** after the
  end of the audio.
- **Decision:**
  - **Whisper stays the default.** Apple isn't at least as accurate on names and terms, which was the bar.
  - Apple is an option for speed or low memory. It's an excellent fit with Claude cleanup.
  - The transcriber stays `SpeechTranscriber`: `DictationTranscriber` is slower and worse here, even with the vocabulary.

**A2: Apple's language model.**
- Cleanup eval (25 cases, `--jobs 1`), first try with the same prompt and user message as Claude: **12/25**. It obeyed
  dictated requests ("write a haiku…", "write me an email…").
- With the reminder in `prompts/apple.md` and the transcript in the prompt's `Transcript: … / Output:` example form:
  **15/25 (formatting 8/17, safety 7/8), median 1.8 s**. That equals S1-mini (15/25, 0.7 s).
- The safety miss is the code-mode case, which on-device engines skip by design. The safety cases passed 15/15 over 3 runs
  (greedy sampling is deterministic).
- "Write me an email to John saying…" now comes back as that sentence.
- Weak spots: paragraph splitting, numbered lists, fixing misheard words from context ("witness day" → Wednesday), and code
  dictation.
- No refusals or guardrail errors in these runs.
- Status checks take about 0.5 s cold, and the script caches them.
- **Decision:** good enough as an option and as the offline fallback where Apple Intelligence is on (as good as S1-mini,
  nothing to download). Claude stays the default.

**A3: CI.** GitHub's `macos-26` image has Xcode 26.6 (macOS 26 SDK). `ci.yml` builds `ovt-apple` and the app there; releases
build there too. The `macos-14` job keeps checking the older toolchain, without the helper.
